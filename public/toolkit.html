Conversation cards

The hardest part of a return is the conversations, not the laptop. These cards give you the words for the moments that matter most.

Two conversations HR often leads. The other cards are on the manager path.

Card 1Return

The “How are you?” call

1–2 months before the return Open card

Why

To reconnect without pressure and hear the parent’s plans.

Ask

  • How are you and the family doing?
  • When are you thinking of coming back, and what would make it easier?
  • How much would you like to hear from us until then?

Avoid

“So, when can we count on you again?”

Card 2Return

The welcome

Day of Return Open card

Why

To set expectations for the first 30 days.

Ask

  • What would make this first week a good one?
  • What would you like to pick up first?
  • Is there anything in your schedule we should plan around?

Avoid

“You’ve missed a lot.” Say instead: “Nobody expects you to know everything today.”

Card 3Reconnect

The weekly check-in

Every week for the first two months, 10 minutes Open card

Why

To catch small problems before they grow.

Ask

  • What went well this week?
  • What was harder than expected?
  • What do you need from me next week?

Avoid

Turning it into a status report.

Card 4Any time

Talking about flexibility

Whenever it comes up Open card

Why

To find hours or a setup that work for both sides.

Ask

  • What would help most right now?
  • Which parts of the role need fixed hours or being on site?
  • Shall we try it for a month and then review?

Avoid

“If I do it for you, I have to do it for everyone.”

Card 5Thrive

The Day 90 review

End of month 3 Open card

Why

To close the return and open the next chapter.

Ask

  • Looking back, what helped and what didn’t?
  • What do you want to own fully from now on?
  • Where do you want to grow in the next year?

Avoid

Talking only about the past. Spend half the time on what comes next.

Card 6Any time

When the child is sick

The morning it happens Open card

Why

How you react that morning is what people remember.

Ask

  • Is everything OK?
  • Do you need anything from us today?
  • What can wait, and what should someone else pick up?

Avoid

A sigh, a pause, or “Again?”

Keep-in-touch email

To a parent on leave

Subject: Thinking of you (no reply needed) Hi [Name], I hope you and the little one are doing well. There are no work questions in this email, just a hello from all of us. A few things you might like to know: [one or two short updates]. If you’d like a short call before you come back, I’m happy to find a time that suits you. If you’d rather hear nothing until then, that’s completely fine too. Warm wishes, [Your name]

Welcome back message

From the team, the day before the Day of Return

Hi [Name], We’re so glad you’re back tomorrow! Everything is ready for you. Coffee is on us at [time], and [buddy’s name] is your go-to person for anything at all. Nobody expects you to know everything on day one. We’re just happy to have you back. See you tomorrow, The [team name] team

Request for flexible working

To your manager or HR

Subject: Request for flexible working arrangements Hi [Name], As I prepare to return on [Day of Return], I’d like to ask about adjusting my working arrangements for [period]. What I’m asking for: [for example, working from home two days a week, starting at 9:30, or part-time hours] Why it helps: [one sentence] How the work stays covered: [one or two sentences] I’d suggest we try it for [one month] and then review it together. Could we talk about it in the next week or two? Thank you, [Your name]

Rights at a glance

The main rules from the Bulgarian Labour Code, as of October 2026.

Leave

Pregnancy and birth leave

410 days per child, 45 of them before the birth. Paid by the NSSI at 90% of average gross insurable income, after at least 12 months of insurance. (Art. 163)

Source: Innovires

Mother
Leave until age 2

After the 410 days, leave until the child turns 2, paid at a fixed monthly amount. A mother who returns to work early keeps part of this benefit. (Art. 164)

Source: Innovires · Rate to be confirmed

Leave at birth

15 days when the child is born.

Father’s leave

2 months, any time until the child turns 8, paid by the NSSI. (Art. 164c)

Source: Trud i Pravo

Once the child is 6 months old, the father can take the rest of the 410 days with the mother’s consent.

Golden rules for parent-friendly teams

Four rules that make the biggest difference, whatever the size of your company.

1. Flexible by default

Allow flexible hours or a hybrid setup in the first weeks, wherever the role allows it.

2. Protect the school run

Avoid key meetings before 9:30 and after 16:30.

3. Ask, don’t assume

Don’t decide for a parent that they won’t want travel, a promotion or a big project. Ask them.

4. Empathy first

Children get sick. How you react that morning is what people remember, and it is what builds loyalty.
